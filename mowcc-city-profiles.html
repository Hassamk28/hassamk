Case Study · Meals on Wheels Collin County

City Impact
Profiles

Data-checked presentations that show each city's leaders what Meals on Wheels does for the seniors in their community. All four are built from a single template.

  • Excel
  • PowerPoint
  • JavaScript
  • Data reconciliation

This is a case study only. Figures in the text are rounded. The slide image uses made-up sample data for a fictional "Sample City."

The problem

Leadership regularly meets with mayors and city officials, who are also important funding partners. Each meeting needs a clear picture of that city: meals delivered over time, seniors served, who those seniors are, and how much volunteer time the community gives. These requests often came with same-day turnaround.

The numbers lived in several places: a meal register going back to 2015, demographic exports, and about three years of monthly volunteer-hour files in slightly different formats. Building each deck by hand was slow and easy to get wrong.

What I built

  1. A volunteer-hours workbook that combines about 36 monthly files into one consistent table, and turns hours into a dollar value of volunteer time.
  2. A reusable 9-slide template generator. I feed it one city's data and it produces a finished, on-brand deck. I used it to build profiles for four of the county's largest cities.
  3. A data brief behind every deck that marks each figure as verified, partial, or unavailable, so leadership knows exactly how solid each number is before they present it.
  4. Reconciliation checks against the source data before anything went out.

City impact profile slide layout (sample data, fictional city)

Results

  • Four city profiles delivered from one template. Each new city is now a data refresh, not a rebuild.
  • The long-term view told a strong story. In one city, annual meals grew about 8× between 2015 and 2024.
  • The figures reconciled to the source within 0.1%, and the checks caught a copy-paste label error before it reached a mayor's desk.

What I learned

  • For outside audiences, being right matters more than being fast. Build the checks into the process so you can be both.
  • Telling people how confident each number is (verified, partial, unavailable) builds more trust than presenting everything as certain.
  • A template turns a recurring scramble into a quick, repeatable task.
